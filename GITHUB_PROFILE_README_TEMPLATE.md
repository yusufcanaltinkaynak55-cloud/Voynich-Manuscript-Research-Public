I investigate how computational methods and AI-assisted research can help us formulate, challenge, and reproduce hypotheses about complex writing systems and semantic structure. My priorities are **testable claims, reproducible evidence, comparative controls, clear attribution, and scientific uncertainty**.

## Research projects

### [Voynich Manuscript Research](https://github.com/yusufcanaltinkaynak55-cloud/Voynich-Manuscript-Research-Public)

An independent, English-language scientific record focused on **Beinecke MS 408**. Its public reference programmes include:

- **B09:** Transformed-spelling collision analysis with source-hash verification and ambiguity controls.
- **B10:** Restricted original-spelling candidate-selection tests with fixed frequency and position baselines.
- **B17-OPEN:** Pairwise word-order analysis with shuffled-training and visible line-position controls.

[![Voynich public quality checks](https://github.com/yusufcanaltinkaynak55-cloud/Voynich-Manuscript-Research-Public/actions/workflows/repository-quality.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/yusufcanaltinkaynak55-cloud/Voynich-Manuscript-Research-Public/actions/workflows/repository-quality.yml)

**Research status:** These are computational measurements of editorial transcriptions, not validated readings or translations. An independently authenticated Voynich decipherment has **not** been established.

### Sherlock (experimental, private)

An AI-assisted computational research programme for exploring written patterns and competing cryptanalytic hypotheses. The proprietary software, private training materials, and unverified candidate keys are **not publicly released**. The public Voynich reference studies are **not** releases of Sherlock itself.

### ALFA / ALFA-Q (experimental)

A research and software-design direction focused on semantic analysis, coherence, information structure, and explainable evaluation. This is ongoing exploratory R&D; it is **not represented as an externally validated product or a formal academic discovery**.

## Research principles

- Design falsifiable hypotheses rather than attach meanings to patterns prematurely.
- Test against simple and strong baselines, disclose failures, and avoid data leakage.
- Credit source authors and prior work.
- Separate statistical regularity, predictive accuracy, cipher calibration, and authenticated historical meaning.
- Invite independent verification.
